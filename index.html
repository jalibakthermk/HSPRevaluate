Method notes

  1. Geometry. Origin on the road at the vehicle centre, x positive to the right, z along the road. Left lamp at −separation/2, right lamp at +separation/2, both at the mounting height. Right-hand traffic; tick Mirror H for a left-hand-traffic beam.
  2. Illuminance is vertical illuminance on a plane facing the vehicle, E = I·cos/d² with cos = z/d, summed over both lamps. Intensity is looked up on a 0.05° grid spanning 45°L–45°R and 15°D–15°U, exactly as the reference software resamples the IES data.
  3. Passing beam. Zones A and B: farthest distance where 1, 3 and 5 lx are exceeded on the road surface along lines 0, 1.5 and 3 m right (B rotated 5° to the right), searched inward from 400 m in 0.5 m steps; 9 values averaged. Zone C: same at 250 mm height, lines 3, 4.5 and 6 m left. Zone D: lateral reach of 3 lx on the road at 30, 40 and 50 m. Zone E: lateral reach of 3 lx at 250 mm height at 10 and 20 m. Flux: 45°L–45°R, 15°D–5°U. Glare: luminous flux from each lamp into a 9.2 × 0.69 m window at 50 m (x −7.9 to +1.3 m, height 0.93–1.62 m) with the 5 × 8 eye-position weights, summed.
  4. Driving beam. Points A–E: farthest distance along a ray from the road origin towards (x, y, 100 m) where 3 lx is exceeded, searched from 800 m: A (0, h), B (−20, h), C (0, h+9), D (+20, h), E (0, h−2), with h the mounting height. Zone E and flux as for the passing beam, flux window 5°D–10°U.
  5. Scoring. Criterion points P = (V − V₁)/(V₂ − V₁) + 1 with V₁ = mean − SD and V₂ = mean + SD of the CIE database (reversed for glare); weighted sum normalised between the 1-point and 5-point benchmarks. EPLB = 5.80·N + 64.17 m, EPHB = 11.71·N + 141.27 m, EPADB = 10.87·N + 94.13 m; system score = 0.626·MEP − 36.42.
  6. IES handling. Type A and B files are used as H/V grids, as the reference does. Type C files are converted, with the polar axis detected from where the peak lies. Candela multiplier and ballast factor are applied (the reference reader ignores them, so results differ from the .exe only when those are not 1). The aim shift moves the beam vertically before evaluation; the HSPR expects the cut-off at the manufacturer's declared aim.
  7. Zone E saturation. At 10 m, 45° from a lamp is only 10 m sideways, so Zone E cannot report much more than 10 m + half the separation. This is why the GTB example shows 10.75 m on both sides for both beams. Light outside ±45° does not count.
